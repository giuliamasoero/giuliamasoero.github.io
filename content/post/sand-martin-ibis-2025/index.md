Many bird species are getting smaller as the climate warms — but not all of them.

In our new paper in __*Ibis*__, Aldo Tamietti and I show that a Sand Martin (*Riparia riparia*) population breeding in northwestern Italy has, over more than two decades of standardised monitoring, __increased__ in both body mass and wing length. This is the opposite of the widely reported "shrinking-bird" pattern.

The result reminds us that the global picture is built from very different local stories, and that long-term, standardised datasets — often maintained by ringers and small ornithological stations — are essential to tell those stories apart from noise.

🔗 Read the paper: [Masoero & Tamietti (2025), *Ibis*, DOI 10.1111/ibi.70020](https://doi.org/10.1111/ibi.70020).
